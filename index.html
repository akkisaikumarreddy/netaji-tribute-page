Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose

1897 – 1945 | A Beacon of Hope & Courage

"Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom!"

Early Life & Principles

Born in Cuttack, Odisha, Subhas Chandra Bose was a brilliant scholar. He proved his academic excellence by securing 4th rank in the prestigious Indian Civil Service (ICS) examination in England in 1920.

Refusing to serve the foreign government, he resigned from service in 1921 to join India's national movement, declaring that no honor was greater than serving one's motherland.

The Visionary Leader

Netaji served as the President of the Indian National Congress in 1938. He advocated for total independence and self-reliance, inspiring youth across the nation to stand strong against oppression.

Azad Hind Fauj & Military Leadership

In 1943, Netaji established the Provisional Government of Free India and reorganized the Azad Hind Fauj (Indian National Army) in Singapore.

He united soldiers across borders with the iconic call "Chalo Delhi!" and established the Rani of Jhansi Regiment—one of the earliest all-women combat units in modern history.

Key Life Milestones

  • 1897: Born on January 23 in Cuttack, Odisha.
  • 1921: Resigned from Civil Services to join the freedom movement.
  • 1938: Elected President of the Indian National Congress.
  • 1939: Formed the All India Forward Bloc.
  • 1943: Formed the Azad Hind Government and led the INA.
  • 1944: Raised the Indian tricolor in liberated territory in Manipur.

An Enduring Legacy

Netaji's courage, strategic brilliance, and supreme sacrifice continue to inspire generations of Indians. His legacy stands as a timeless symbol of patriotism and fearlessness.
